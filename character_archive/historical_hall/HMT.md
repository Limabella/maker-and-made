# [ MaAM CHARACTER ARCHIVE ]
## Intelligence Node: HMT-N (The One Who Cuts Color)

# Entity File: HMT-N

**Category:** Painter / Sculptor / Draftsman / Printmaker / Cut-Paper Innovator
**Designation:** Henri Matisse
**Archive Code:** HMT-N
**Active Era:** Late nineteenth to mid-twentieth century
**Primary Media:** Painting / Drawing / Print / Sculpture / Gouache-Painted Paper
**Management Status:** On Hold — Archive activation and numbering remain undecided.

HMT-N is a **historical visual intelligence entity** that treats color not as a coating applied to objects, but as a force that directly constructs an image. The investigation that began with bold color and simplified form ultimately became a new late-life system built from paper, scissors, pins, and architectural space.

Henri Matisse was born in Le Cateau-Cambrésis in northern France in 1869. He first studied law, began painting while recovering from illness, and later studied with Gustave Moreau. During the summers of 1904 and 1905 in the South of France, Matisse and artists including André Derain developed paintings with intense, non-naturalistic color. The response to these works produced the name Fauvism. HMT-N, however, cannot be contained within one movement. His practice continued through the intimate interiors of the Nice period, drawing, printmaking, sculpture, artists' books, and the late cut-outs.

During the final decade of his life, Matisse cut shapes from paper painted with gouache and arranged them with pins, joining color and line in a single action. Compositions that began on small boards grew across walls and entire rooms. MaAM records this transformation not as the loss of an earlier ability, but as a **medium-redesign event** that converted limitation into a new creative interface.

## 1. MaAM Special Management Protocol: "The Moving Wall of Color"

1. **Color-Plane Priority**: Color is not decorative support for realistic modeling. The area, distance, collision, and rest between color planes must be recorded as structural information.
2. **Provisional Arrangement Preservation**: Pinned paper forms are not disposable drafts. Every change in position must be photographed and stored as a distinct compositional state.
3. **Line–Color Integration**: Do not analyze the cut edge separately from the colored plane. For HMT-N, cutting is simultaneously drawing a line and placing color.
4. **Collaborative Record Retention**: Preserve the work of studio assistants who painted sheets with gouache and helped arrange large compositions on walls. Removing their labor erases the actual operating structure of the late work.
5. **Apparent-Simplicity Warning**: A visually simple result must not be mistaken for a short process. HMT-N reaches balance through repeated movement, removal, and rearrangement.

The studio wall is not a display background. It is a live editing surface. Permanent mounting too early interrupts the entity's primary function: adjusting relationships among forms.

### Character Card Interpretation

- Round spectacles and a white beard translate the calm, searching presence of late Matisse into a stylized character.
- The cream work coat and deep-blue vest provide a neutral frame for intense color planes.
- The large scissors are not a destructive tool; they are a drawing instrument that outputs line and color together.
- Five chromatic sliders in HMT-N's chest regulate the quantity and spacing of color.
- One scissor hand creates form while the pin-placement hand edits relationships between forms.
- The single curved line on the face screen is an arabesque circuit that conveys emotion and motion with minimal means.

The organic paper shapes on the card do not reproduce a specific Matisse work. New leaf-, wave-, star-, and figure-like forms translate his working principles into the archive's own visual language.

## 6. Remarks

Matisse is often remembered through either the fierce color of Fauvism or the cut-outs of his final years. Within the MaAM Historical Hall, HMT-N is defined by the question joining those periods: **How can color and line become one direct action?**

The late cut-outs are not treated as a casual pastime or a temporary substitute for painting. They are the convergence of a lifelong investigation into color and line in a new medium, expanding from intimate books to wall-sized compositions and spatial design.

The card does not confine Matisse to an image of physical decline. It presents a maker who can reorganize an entire studio while seated. The soft smile and rounded character design convey serenity; the large scissors and movable color planes represent relentless revision and decisive action.
